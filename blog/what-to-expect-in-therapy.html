What to Expect in Therapy: A Guide for First-Timers

If you've never been to therapy before, it's completely normal to feel unsure about what to expect.

You might be wondering:

"What if I don't know what to say?"
"Will it be awkward?"
"What if I cry?"

If any of these questions have crossed your mind, you're not alone. Therapy can feel like stepping into the unknown, especially if it's your first time. My hope is that by the end of this article, you'll have a clearer picture of what the process actually looks like, so taking that first step feels a little less intimidating.

Before Your First Session

Before booking your first appointment, many therapists, including myself, offer a free introductory call. This is usually a brief 15-minute conversation where we can get to know each other, discuss what has brought you to therapy, and answer any questions you might have about the process.

Think of it less as an assessment and more as an opportunity to see whether working together feels like the right fit. You don't need to prepare anything in advance or know exactly what to say. Some people already have a clear idea of what they'd like to work on, while others simply know that something doesn't feel quite right. Both are completely okay.

Most importantly, there's no pressure to commit afterwards. The conversation is simply there to help you make an informed decision.

The First Session

One of the biggest misconceptions about therapy is that you'll be expected to share your whole life story in the very first appointment. In reality, the first session is usually much gentler than people imagine.

The first session is about getting to know you; what has brought you to therapy, what's been happening in your life recently, and what you hope to gain from the process. If you're not entirely sure what your goals are yet, that's okay too. Many people begin therapy simply knowing they want things to feel different.

The session will also cover practical aspects of therapy, including confidentiality, what to expect from the therapeutic relationship, and how future sessions may look. You're encouraged to ask questions and make sure you feel comfortable with the process before deciding how you'd like to move forward.

Ongoing Sessions

As therapy continues, sessions often begin to find a natural rhythm. There isn't a set agenda that every therapist follows, and no two sessions are exactly the same. What each session looks like will depend on what's happening in your life and what feels most important to talk about that day.

Some weeks you might want to talk about something that happened yesterday. Other weeks you may find yourself reflecting on patterns that have been present for years. Sometimes you'll leave feeling lighter. Other times you may leave with something new to think about. There isn't a "perfect" therapy session, and progress doesn't always happen in obvious ways.

Therapy is a collaborative process rather than a place where someone simply tells you what to do. Instead, it provides an opportunity to explore your experiences, gain new perspectives, and work towards meaningful change at a pace that feels right for you.

A Few Things That May Help to Know

You don't need to have everything figured out.

Many people worry that they won't know what to talk about or that they need to explain everything perfectly. The truth is, there are no "right" words in therapy. It's okay if your thoughts feel messy or if you're not sure where to begin — we can work through that together.

You don't have to be in crisis.

Therapy isn't only for people experiencing severe mental health difficulties. Many people come because they're feeling overwhelmed, navigating a life transition, struggling in relationships, or simply wanting to understand themselves better. You don't need to wait until things become unbearable before reaching out for support.

It's okay if building trust takes time.

Talking about personal experiences with someone you've only just met can feel unfamiliar. There's no expectation that you'll feel completely comfortable from the very first session. Building a trusting relationship takes time, and therapy moves at a pace that feels safe and manageable for you.

You can change therapists if it's not the right fit.

The therapeutic relationship matters enormously. If something doesn't feel right after giving it a genuine chance, it's okay to seek someone else. A good therapist will support you in doing so.

Ready to Take the First Step?

If you've been thinking about therapy but weren't sure what to expect, hopefully this article has given you a clearer picture of what the process can look like. While every therapist works a little differently, the goal is the same: to create a space where you feel heard, understood, and supported.

If you're considering therapy and would like to explore whether it's the right fit for you, a free 15-minute introductory call can be a helpful place to start. It's an opportunity to ask questions, discuss what you're looking for, and decide whether you'd feel comfortable working together without any pressure or obligation.
